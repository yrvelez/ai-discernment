# Reviewer pass (automated, single pass)

Model: `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5`. The arm-level numbers in the prose match the tables, but the report presents a post hoc reconstruction as 'planned' with 'supported' flags, leaves the 227 excluded rows unexplained, and leans on a pooled estimate it admits is unreliable.

- **high** — Design and data / sample flow: The raw N is 2,257 and the analysis N is 2,030, yet 'exclusions []' is listed. 227 rows (about 10%) are dropped with no stated criteria, no breakdown by arm, and no check that attrition is balanced across arms. The weight variable `pr` (the basis of the 1/pr weights) is also never defined.
  - Suggested fix: State the exclusion rules (attention checks, incompleteness, duplicates) and give the number dropped per rule and per arm. Define `pr` and say whether it is a design or a post-stratification weight.
- **high** — H1–H3 headers and 'Registered versus implemented' table: The hypotheses are labelled 'PLANNED' and carry 'Registered direction', 'Supported' and 'as planned' fields, although no registration exists and the plan was reconstructed from the analysis script. The registered/implemented comparison is therefore circular: the 'plan' is the implementation, and the 'unregistered' tag sits beside 'as planned'. A reader could take the 'Supported: yes' flags as confirmatory.
  - Suggested fix: Rename these as post hoc / exploratory analyses and replace 'Supported' with 'p<0.05 (uncorrected)'. Drop or caveat the registered-vs-implemented table and the 'Registered direction' wording. State clearly that H1–H3 were derived from the script.
- **high** — Summary / H2 pooled estimate: The headline pooled H2 effect (+0.038, p=0.0004) comes from a random-effects pooling that the report itself says treats arms sharing one control group as independent, so its SE and p are approximate. It is still described as the pooled effect with no hedging. In addition, no individual arm survives a Bonferroni threshold over 33 tests (0.05/33≈0.0015; the smallest arm p is 0.0027), and the summary does not say so.
  - Suggested fix: Report a pooled effect that accounts for the shared control (e.g., all treated vs. control in one regression). Say explicitly that no arm-level result survives multiplicity correction. Describe the p=0.0004 as unreliable.
- **medium** — H3 text: The text says 'The pooled H3 estimate is not among the provided tables', but the H3 section reports it directly (-0.007, SE 0.010, p=0.502; tau²=0.0004, I²=0.40). The H1 and H2 prose both discuss their pooled estimates, so this is an internal inconsistency.
  - Suggested fix: Report the pooled H3 estimate in the prose, consistent with H1 and H2, and delete the 'not provided' sentence.
- **medium** — H1 results tables (mean_treated vs estimate): Raw arm means are close to the control mean of 0.693 for several flagged arms: Mindfulness 0.696, Guide 0.699, Breathing 0.696. The adjusted estimates are -0.039 for Mindfulness and +0.047 for Guide. For Mindfulness the raw difference is slightly positive while the adjusted effect is significantly negative, a sign reversal. The mean columns are not described (weighted or unweighted), and the weighted intercept of 0.698 differs from the stated control mean of 0.693. The flagged Mindfulness and Guide results may depend on the Lin covariate adjustment or on the weights.
  - Suggested fix: Say whether the raw means are weighted. Report unadjusted, weighted-only and Lin-adjusted estimates, and explain why adjustment flips the Mindfulness sign. Treat the Mindfulness and Guide findings as fragile.
- **medium** — Interpretation of H2 vs H3: The study is framed as improving 'discernment', but the outcomes are separate accuracy shares on fake and real items, with no sensitivity (d′) or bias measure. Higher fake-item accuracy with flat or lower real-item accuracy (e.g., Breathing +0.091 on fake, -0.048 on real) could just reflect a shift toward guessing 'AI'. The report never says this.
  - Suggested fix: Add a discernment index or signal-detection measure (hit rate vs. false-alarm rate). At minimum, note that the fake-item gains may reflect response bias rather than better discrimination.
- **low** — Design and data / reproducibility: The report says the plan is 'transcribed verbatim' from an R script, but the model formulas use Python/statsmodels syntax (`C(arm_code, Treatment(reference='0'))`). It also cites `scripts/02_clean.py`. It is unclear which code produced the results. The number of covariates and the centering of `media_trust_c`, `political_interest_c`, `pk_score_c` and `ai_scale_c` are not described, and the item count per respondent is not given.
  - Suggested fix: State which code actually generated the results and how the covariates were centered and constructed. Give the number of items and the number of fake vs. real items per respondent.
- **low** — Related findings: The literature note contains a '[citation removed: not in retrieved set]' placeholder, the Ouyang et al. entry has a title that does not match the cited paper, and the last reference is truncated ('A survey on large'). The section adds little to the argument.
  - Suggested fix: Repair or remove the broken citations and the placeholder, or shorten the section to the one or two relevant works.
