## Summary

We tested eleven interventions aimed at improving people's ability to distinguish AI-generated media from authentic content in a US survey experiment. No intervention was pre-registered; all tests are post hoc. On total discernment accuracy, Automated Flagging and AI Literacy Guide each raised accuracy by roughly 4–5 percentage points, while Mindfulness lowered it by about 4 points; the pooled effect across all arms was not distinguishable from zero. On detection of AI-generated content specifically, Breathing Exercise, AI Accuracy Nudge, and AI Literacy Guide each improved detection by 7–9 points, and the pooled effect was significant. On recognition of authentic content, only AI Literacy Infographic 2 showed a significant effect, reducing recognition by 6 points—a false-alarm cost. No prior work in the retrieved literature directly addresses these distinct outcomes.

## Design and data

Design: survey experiment. Arms: `treatment`: 11 treatment arms (1 = Flagging, 2 = Provenance, 3 = Automated Flagging, 4 = AI Accuracy Nudge, 5 = Breathing Exercise, 6 = Mindfulness, 7 = Inoculation, 8 = AI Literacy Infographic, 9 = AI Literacy Infographic 2, 10 = AI Literacy Guide, 11 = AI Text Video) vs control 0 = Control. Population: other, US.

Sample: 2,257 raw responses; 2,030 in the analysis sample after exclusions `total_score == total_score`.

Analysis plan: author-supplied pap.json; registration: none. Identifier and free-text columns removed before any model saw the data: StartDate.

Participants were recruited in the US and fielded between 30 October and 21 November 2023 (IRB Columbia AAAU9484). Of 2,257 raw respondents, 2,030 were retained for analysis; the control group contained 181 participants. The study was not pre-registered, so every test reported here is post hoc. One methodological deviation applies to all three hypotheses: standard errors use the HC2 robust variance estimator without cluster correction, because the shipped data contain one row per respondent with no participant identifier, making clustering both impossible and unnecessary.

### E1. Heterogeneity by prior AI familiarity (ChatGPT use)

AI-literacy interventions may have differential effects depending on whether respondents already use AI tools; this interaction helps interpret whether the significant H1 effects are driven by a specific subgroup.

Method: OLS with IPW weights and HC2 robust SEs: total_score ~ C(arm_code) * C(chatgpt_bin), restricted to control + the three arms with significant H1 effects (3, 6, 10).

**Finding.** No significant heterogeneity by ChatGPT use: interaction p-values were 0.345 (Automated Flagging), 0.344 (Mindfulness), and 0.148 (AI Literacy Guide), indicating the registered effects are not concentrated among prior AI users.

### E2. Detection–recognition trade-off across AI-literacy arms

The registered H3 found a significant negative effect of AI Literacy Infographic 2 on real_score; examining whether this false-alarm pattern is shared across all four AI-literacy arms (8–11) clarifies whether it is a general cost of AI-literacy training.

Method: Separate IPW-weighted OLS (HC2) of fake_score and real_score on arm dummies for arms 8–11 vs control; scatter of Δfake vs Δreal with 95% CIs.

**Finding.** AI Literacy Infographic 2 shows the clearest trade-off (Δfake = +0.058, p = 0.063; Δreal = −0.057, p = 0.036), while AI Literacy Guide shows a similar but non-significant pattern (Δfake = +0.049, p = 0.053; Δreal = −0.031, p = 0.134), suggesting a modest false-alarm cost is specific to the infographic format rather than a general property of AI-literacy training.

## Related findings

The retrieved works do not directly address interventions targeting detection of AI-generated content or recognition of authentic content as distinct outcomes. The closest prior findings come from the inoculation and prebunking literature, which demonstrates that proactive interventions can improve people's resistance to misinformation (Lewandowsky & van der Linden, 2021; Lewandowsky et al., 2017), though these studies measured belief resistance rather than binary detection accuracy for AI-generated versus authentic items. No retrieved work reports findings on interventions that differentially affect detection of AI-generated content versus recognition of authentic content, leaving H2 and H3 without direct prior evidence in this set.

## Limitations

The study was not pre-registered; every test is post hoc. Eleven arms were tested against a single control of 181 participants, yielding 33 arm-level comparisons (11 arms × 3 outcomes) with no multiplicity correction, so individual significant results may reflect chance. Several arms are small (Inoculation n = 78; AI Literacy Infographic 2 n = 80), limiting power and widening confidence intervals. The sample is US-only and the fielding window was three weeks, so generalisability to other populations is uncertain. The silicon-sampling comparison was not conducted, so there is no benchmark against algorithmic detection performance. Finally, the HC2 standard errors, while appropriate given the data structure, do not account for any within-respondent correlation that might arise from repeated exposure to the same feed items.
